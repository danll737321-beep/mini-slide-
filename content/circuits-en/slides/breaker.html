BONUS: IS IT BECAUSE OF SERIES? NO!

Why does a breaker trip?

Cause 1: too much at once

Home appliances are in parallel. Each one adds a branch, so the total current goes up.

Running together (120 V)Current
Hair dryer, 1500 W12.5 A
Electric kettle, 1200 W10 A
Total: over the 20 A limit, trips22.5 A

Cause 2: a short circuit

Two wires touch, the current skips the appliance, and resistance drops to almost zero.

I = V ÷ R
R ≈ 0 → I spikes

The breaker cuts power before the wires overheat.

Appliances are in parallel, so each switches on its own. The breaker is in series with the whole circuit, so when it trips, everything on that circuit goes off.

Circuits & Ohm’s Law · 13

Many students think breakers trip because of series wiring, or because “there isn’t enough electricity.” Both are wrong: the outlet supplies a fixed voltage, and the appliances decide how much current flows. To convert watts to amps, divide by the voltage: 1500 ÷ 120 = 12.5 A. The wattages are typical values; check each appliance’s label. 15 A and 20 A are common limits for a single US household circuit.
