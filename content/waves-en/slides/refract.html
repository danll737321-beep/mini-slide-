Refraction: light bends

Solid: what you see. Faint dashes: where the chopstick really is.

When light passes from water into air, it changes speed, so its direction bends. That’s refraction.

That’s why the chopstick looks broken at the surface, even though it’s perfectly straight.

More examples: a pool looks shallower than it is; glasses and magnifiers use refraction to bend light.

Waves, Sound & Light · 11

Put a pencil or chopstick in a clear glass of water and look from the side. Remind students: reflection is light bouncing back; refraction is light going through but changing direction. Lenses work by refraction.
