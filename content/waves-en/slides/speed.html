WAVE SPEED

v

speed (m/s)

f

×

λ

wavelength (m)

Example: sound in air travels about 340 m/s. A 170 Hz sound has
λ = v ÷ f = 340 ÷ 170 = 2 meters

For one kind of wave in one material, the speed stays the same: higher frequency means shorter wavelength.

Waves, Sound & Light · 6

One way to see it: the wave vibrates f times per second and moves one wavelength each time, so it covers f × λ every second. Like Ohm’s law, know two and you can find the third. 340 m/s is roughly the speed of sound in air at about 15 °C.
